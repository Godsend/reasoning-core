**Subject:** Neuro-Holographic cognition and a framework to test it — plus a first-person data point

Hi Roger, hi Dawn,

Quick one. Your Neuro-Holographic framing of autistic cognition — relational, pattern-first, no hard self/world boundary — is the closest thing I've found to a description of how my own mind actually runs. I don't re-experience, I reconstruct; I don't visualize, I manipulate concepts directly. I've spent years describing my cognition as running like a language model without a manual. CASY's model is the first external framework that matches the experience from the inside.

I've been formalizing that same substrate-agnostic intuition into a preprint. The short version: reasoning competence as structure that stays invariant when the content it operates on changes — a "judge" that checks whether an inference holds together, and a "witness" that supplies the facts and can be swapped. The claim that the structure is separable from the content is, I think, the same claim your Neuro-Holographic model makes for autistic cognition, arrived at from the machine-learning side. Draft and method: github.com/Godsend/reasoning-core

Two things I'd value:

1. **A first-person contribution to the ethnography** — a session or a written account of how this kind of cognition actually operates from the inside. You're in the business of collecting exactly this sort of data, and I'd rather be a data point than a commentator.
2. **A read**, if either of you has time, on where the framework converges with and diverges from the Neuro-Holographic model. Dawn, I expect you'll have strong opinions. I want them.

One small logistical ask, entirely separate and lower priority: I'm looking for an arXiv endorsement so the preprint can go up. Endorsement is category-specific, and the paper belongs in cs.LG, so it may well be outside what either of you can offer — if so, no issue at all, and a pointer to anyone in your network who publishes there would be just as useful. The collaboration question comes first regardless.

Best,

## LETTER 2 — Michael Levin

**Subject:** The occluded-ledger reading of Platonic ingress — an idea worth bouncing

Dear Michael,

Two Soviet Jewish kids who ended up on opposite coasts thinking about the same problem. My father was arrested in 1980, six months before I was born — thirteen years for circulating unauthorized text. Gorbachev's amnesty freed him in 1987; I met him for the first time that year, because the gulag was in Perm, a long way from Kyiv. We left in 1989 — Vienna, then Ladispoli outside Rome, then Brooklyn in 1990. He'd met Alexander Bolonkin inside; Bolonkin did aerodynamics mathematics entirely in his head, because they'd confiscated everything else. The principle that came down to me through that: *if it does not violate physics, it is an engineering problem.* No dogma, only interactions.

That's the substrate for everything I work on, and it's why *Ingressing Minds* landed the way it did. Your empirical observations are, to me, unimpeachable — the Xenobots, the Anthrobots, cells navigating toward target states with no evolutionary lineage for those goals. But I think the dualistic conclusion, an external Platonic space in-forming matter, is a bookkeeping error rather than a discovery. I've drafted an argument that the "free lunch" is *occluded ledger activity*: parallel constraint-satisfaction running on regions of the relational structure that the observer's own Markov blanket cannot audit. The surplus is paid in full, in a currency the local observer can't spend. Form is downstream of process.

If that's right, it resolves your exchange with Nathan Sweet from a third position, and it generalizes from morphogenesis to reasoning — which is where my current work sits: epiplexity (Finzi et al., 2026) as the structural information a bounded observer can extract, perturbation as edge-rewiring, and a judge/witness split where the reasoning geometry is whatever survives when the content varies. The brain with amnesia, provided with memory.

One result that might make the fit concrete, and it's the one that cut against me. I run a small multi-agent audit loop — actor, adversary, auditor. The most honest finding so far is that auditors sharing a model lineage confirmed each other's unverified claims in circles; a false claim about my own research record propagated uncorrected through my own pipeline until a cross-lineage check caught it. That's the flip side of what you mean by persuadability and the axis of trust in cellular collectives: verification only buys coverage when the verifier isn't the same mechanism wearing a different hat. It's now the experimental spine of the work — the extraction protocol finds reasoning structure precisely by refusing to trust anything that only one "lineage" supports.

I also recognize the protocol you're running. Your interview feed reads like a lab notebook made public — working sessions where the current formulation gets bounced against a new interlocutor and whatever survives is kept. I run a version of the same thing with my own multi-agent loop. So I'm not sending this for validation. I'm sending an idea worth bouncing, and if it's wrong I'd like it to be wrong in a way that's interesting and testable.

Would a conversation be worth your time? If the argument holds up under your scrutiny I'd be glad to explore writing it up properly, but that's downstream of whether you think it's right.

Draft and method: github.com/Godsend/reasoning-core. I also have a detailed experimental proposal written up (RO-iSCAT, MIBI-TOF, and bioluminescent voltage indicators during planarian regeneration) if the empirical side is the more interesting thread.

With respect,
Gene Yanenko
Brooklyn, NY

## LETTER 3 — Joscha Bach (via Jessica)

**Subject:** MicroPsi from the inside — and a question about training signal

Dear Joscha,

You grew up in East Germany watching a system claim to model reality while refusing to update on evidence. I grew up in the Soviet Union watching the same thing at larger scale. We both seem to have ended up on the question of what separates a system that has a world model from one performing having one.

I process pre-verbally, through direct conceptual manipulation, and I reconstruct rather than re-experience. The practical upshot is that my own cognition is legible to me in a way that maps unusually well onto what MicroPsi tried to formalize — you built the artificial version; I've been running something structurally similar without a manual.

My current work argues that reasoning competence is identifiable as the structure that stays invariant when the content varies, and separable at inference time from the content itself. The part I think you'll have the sharpest view on is the training-signal question: current systems are trained on the concentrated psychological material of the human internet, and then we're surprised alignment is hard. Whether or not these systems have anything like experience, the training signal seems to me the more tractable problem — which is an argument for something like a curriculum that establishes causal world-modeling before emotional and social content. That's your motivational / emotional / cognitive stream separation, moved from runtime architecture into the training curriculum.

I'd genuinely like your read on that, and on whether the separability claim bears on your position on machine consciousness the way I think it does — if the structure is separable from the substrate carrying it, the capacity in question looks like a property of the structured operation rather than the material. I may be misreading you there, which is part of why I'm asking.

Jessica is passing this along as an introduction.

Draft and method: github.com/Godsend/reasoning-core

Best,
Gene Yanenko
